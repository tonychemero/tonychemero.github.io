Anthony Chemero

Philosophy and cognitive science · Vanderbilt University

Anthony Chemero is Professor of Philosophy and Connected Computing at Vanderbilt University. Chemero’s research is both philosophical and empirical; typically, it tries to be both at the same time. He focuses on questions related to nonlinear dynamical modeling, ecological psychology, artificial intelligence, phenomenology, and social cognition.

Before coming to Vanderbilt in 2026, Chemero was Distinguished Research Professor of Philosophy and Psychology at the University of Cincinnati (2012–2026), and before that Professor of Scientific and Philosophical Studies of Mind at Franklin & Marshall College (1999–2014). He holds a Ph.D. in Philosophy and Cognitive Science from Indiana University (1999) and a B.A. in Philosophy from Tufts University (1991).

Teaching

Chemero has taught across philosophy, psychology, and cognitive science at Franklin & Marshall, Cincinnati, and Vanderbilt, from introductory logic, ethics, and philosophy of mind to seminars on ecological psychology, enactivism, phenomenology, artificial life, and art and cognitive science. He has directed thirteen doctoral dissertations, mentored four postdoctoral fellows, and supervised dozens of undergraduate research projects.

In the fall of 2026 he is teaching one graduate seminar: The Social Mind.
